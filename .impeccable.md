# Design context

Captured from the project brief rather than a questionnaire: the direction below
was given explicitly by the author while the app was being built.

## Users

One person, posting live from inside an event: a conference talk, a match, a
launch, a breaking story. They are half-watching something else and typing with
one hand. Often the window is a narrow column beside a live stream, or a phone
held at an awkward angle in a crowd. Being first matters to them.

The job: get a short observation onto Bluesky, in the right thread, faster than
the official app allows, and see whether it landed.

## Brand personality

**Plain, quick, unbranded.** It should feel like a part of the operating system
rather than a product with opinions. No mascot, no marketing voice, no
onboarding. The interface earns its place by disappearing.

Emotionally: calm under pressure. Nothing should flash, bounce or congratulate.

## Aesthetic direction

- **Neutral only.** Blacks, greys and whites in both themes. Colour is reserved
  for two states that must be noticed: errors, and the dot that says a thread is
  live. Primary actions invert the page rather than introducing a brand hue.
- **System typeface, deliberately.** The usual advice is to avoid system font
  stacks; here it is the point. The app should read as native chrome on macOS,
  Windows, Android and iOS alike. No webfont is loaded, which also removes a
  render-blocking request from a tool used on venue wifi.
- **Lucide icons** (ISC), one family, one stroke weight.
- **Anti-references:** the Bluesky app's blue, anything with a gradient, anything
  that announces itself. An earlier draft copied Bluesky's palette and was
  rejected for exactly that reason.
- **Theme follows the operating system**, overridable in settings. Both themes
  are first-class: the same thread gets posted from a dark room and a bright
  street.

## Design principles

1. **The composer never moves.** It sits under the newest post, one line tall,
   growing downwards. The caret returns to it after every send. Everything else
   in the layout arranges itself around that fact.
2. **Latency is a design material.** Blobs upload on drop, alt text is written
   in the background, the box clears before the network call returns. Perceived
   speed is the feature.
3. **Chrome appears on demand.** Row controls fade in on hover or selection;
   counters appear once there is something to count. A resting screen shows
   posts and a box, nothing else.
4. **Keyboard first, touch second, mouse third.** Every action has a key. Every
   target that survives to touch is at least 40px.
5. **British English throughout, no em dashes.** Sentence case for labels.
